# Results so far

Editing two MLP matrices removes reasoning loops from small Qwen3.5 models in thinking mode
and raises their benchmark accuracy. Method: `.claude/skills/weight-edit/SKILL.md`; per-case details, controls and
variants: `cases/*/RESULTS.md` (generated) and `cases/*/NOTES.md` (decisions); lessons: `playbook.md`.

Paired (same problems and per-problem seeds), model-card thinking sampling, 32,768 new tokens; SE clustered by
problem. Generated by `python -m wsp.case report`.

## The edit
For each chosen layer: `W_down -= alpha * u (u^T W_down)[:, top-k]`, where `u` is the residual-stream direction
separating loop continuations from normal reasoning (mean difference over matched pairs from the model's own
correct traces) and the top-k columns are the MLP neurons writing most along `u` in loop contexts. Both cases use
layers 15 and 19, k = 64, alpha = 6; the qwen35-0.8b-think_loop case independently reproduces the post's result
for that model.

## What it does and does not do
- It removes an obstacle, not a lack of ability: before the edit, answers that finished were almost all correct;
  the gain is answers that now finish. Models without such an obstacle (see `cases/screening/RESULTS.md`) cannot be
  helped this way.
- It does not just force early stopping: correct answers get longer, because harder problems now finish.
- The norm-matched random-direction control is null in both cases; selectivity on held-out pairs predicted which
  variants would hurt.

## Caveats
- The obstacle exists under the model card's thinking settings with a 32k budget; baselines are weak because of
  loops. Non-weight fixes (repetition penalty, budget forcing) are not compared yet.
- Practice problems (selection, holdout) come from GSM8K / MATH train.
